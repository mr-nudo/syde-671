Bringing 100-Year-Old Glass Plates to Life

An Automated Image Alignment Pipeline for the Prokudin-Gorskii Collection

1. The Goal

In the early 1900s, Russian photographer Sergei Mikhailovich Prokudin-Gorskii captured high-definition full-color images by taking three consecutive glass plate photographs through Blue, Green, and Red filters.

Because the camera shifted slightly between exposures, stacking these three plates directly creates severe color fringing. Our goal is to write a Python algorithm that automatically aligns the plates into a sharp, beautiful RGB composite!

2. Step 1: The Simple Approach (Single-Scale Search)

My first attempt was a basic exhaustive search: keep the Blue channel fixed as our anchor, and slide Green and Red over a small window of [-15, 15] pixels along $x$ and $y$.

To measure alignment quality, I evaluated both L2 Distance (MSE) and Normalized Cross-Correlation (NCC). NCC performed significantly better because it normalizes brightness differences across different color channels.

Results on Low-Res JPGs:

  • Speed: Super fast! Low-resolution images (like cathedral.jpg) aligned in just ~0.4 seconds.
  • Quality: Perfectly crisp alignment for small displacements.
Aligned Cathedral (Single-Scale) | Offset: G(0, 4), R(0, 13)

3. Step 2: Hitting a Wall with High-Res Images & The Pyramid Fix

When I ran the single-scale algorithm on high-resolution images (like 01725u.jpg), it completely failed! The displacement needed was well over 100 pixels, so the [-15, 15] search window hit a boundary wall and stopped at (15, 15).

Expanding the search window to [-200, 200] on a 60MB TIFF would take several minutes per image.

The Solution: Multi-Scale Image Pyramid

Instead, I built an Image Pyramid:

  1. Recursively downsample the image by a factor of 2 until it's small (< 128px).
  2. Find the coarse shift at the smallest scale.
  3. Scale the estimated shift back up by $2\times$ at each ascending level and refine locally using a tight [-7, 7] window.

The Result: High-resolution images that previously failed now aligned in just ~13–14 seconds!

4. Improvement Tricks (Bells & Whistles)

When aligning different color filters, raw pixel intensity matching can fail (e.g., a bright blue sky looks dark in a red filter plate). To solve this, I added two key tweaks:

  • Edge Features (Sobel Gradient): Instead of matching raw pixel values, the algorithm aligns the structural edges computed via Sobel filters.
  • Border Cropping: Ignored 15% of the outer borders during distance evaluation so black glass margins don't corrupt the score.
